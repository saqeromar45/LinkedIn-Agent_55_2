# 2026-09-05 — Mundra empty-container disruption

**Angle:** Mundra Port empty-yard / trailer-operator disruption after APSEZ restricted empty nominations to SEZ-designated yards (effective 1 Sept 2026). ~5,000 containers/day estimated held up; empty allotments for factory stuffing and empty gate-ins suspended; disruption widens toward laden pickup. Hits Indian Subcontinent origin already among the tightest globally (service withdrawals, equipment shortages, 3–6 week booking windows). Distinct from Dutch port/rail strikes (Europe gateway labor) and prior Asia typhoon / Panama themes.

**Sources:** India Shipping News / Tradlinx (Mundra yard strike); C.H. Robinson Sep 2026 ocean update (ISC tightness); Kuehne+Nagel port ops update 26 Aug / early Sept Mundra advisories.

## Post copy

```
At Mundra, the bottleneck isn’t the vessel — it’s the empty box.

India’s largest container gateway is under pressure after empty-yard operators halted work over new SEZ-only depot rules. Industry estimates put roughly 5,000 containers a day at risk as empty allotments for factory stuffing and empty gate-ins were suspended. When empties stop moving, exports don’t leave — even if the ship still has space.

That hits an origin already among the world’s tightest. Across the Indian Subcontinent, service withdrawals, port omissions, equipment shortages and tighter carrier allocations have stretched booking windows to about four weeks for North America and three to six weeks for Europe. Extra transshipment stops can fill gaps, but each connection adds delay risk just as peak-season cargo still needs reliable slots.

If your India or Bangladesh program still treats equipment availability as a given — which sailings are you protecting with earlier empty requests, alternate yards, or dual-gateway plans this month?
```
