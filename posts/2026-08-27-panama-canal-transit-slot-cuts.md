# Panama Canal daily transit slot cuts — 2026-08-27

**Angle:** ACP Advisory (Aug 20) cuts daily transit capacity from 36 → 34 (Neopanamax 9 / Panamax 25 from Sep 3) then → 32 (Panamax 23 from Sep 15) as watershed rainfall runs ~34% below average. Separately, from Sep 3 daily auction slots split into four cargo groups (LNG/LPG; dry bulk/general; container/RoRo/reefer; tankers). Neopanamax allocation prioritizes full containerships with highest TEU. Draft cuts postponed; this is a *capacity* story, not the Aug 9 draft theme. Queues and auction premiums already elevated (reports of multi-million Neopanamax auction bids).

Draft limits were the last Panama story. Capacity is the next one.

The Panama Canal Authority is cutting daily transit slots — from 36 to 34 starting September 3, then to 32 from September 15 — as watershed rainfall runs well below normal. That is fewer sailings through both Neopanamax and Panamax locks, not just lighter loads. From September 3, daily auction capacity is also split into four cargo groups: gas carriers, dry bulk, containers/RoRo/reefer, and tankers. Full containerships with the highest TEU get Neopanamax priority. Unreserved arrivals already face multi-day waits; auction premiums have climbed into the millions for a single slot.

For Asia–U.S. East Coast and energy shippers, the operational question is no longer only draft and payload. It is whether your booking is locked, which cargo segment you compete in for auction space, and whether West Coast or Cape diversion is cheaper than paying for certainty. Peak season is arriving into a canal that is rationing water by rationing days.

If your Panama plan still assumes 36 daily slots and an open auction pool — what reservation and diversion playbook are you confirming before September 3?
