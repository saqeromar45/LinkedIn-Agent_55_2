# 2026-08-05 — Hormuz chokepoint: energy, fertilizer & industrial shock

**Angle:** Strait of Hormuz disruption (ITC/UN analysis) cuts far beyond oil — LNG ~95% down, urea/methanol/ammonia 75–83% down, plastics/aluminium hit — while trade diversion has not fully replaced lost volumes and Red Sea risk still limits clean reroutes.

**Image:** `assets/hormuz-chokepoint-shipping.png`

**Status:** published as `urn:li:share:7490709806953971712`

**Short copy:**
```
One strait. A quarter of the world’s seaborne oil. A third of globally traded urea. And export volumes through it just fell by more than half.

Fresh ITC analysis — carried by UN trade channels — shows how disruption in the Strait of Hormuz has cut far beyond crude. LNG exports collapsed by roughly 95%. Urea, methanol, and ammonia plunged 75–83%. Plastics and aluminium moved with them. This isn’t only a fuel-price story. It’s a multi-commodity shock hitting energy, fertilizer, and industrial inputs at the same time.

What makes this harder is the thinness of Plan B. Trade diversion has started, but it has not fully replaced lost Hormuz volumes. With Red Sea risk still elevated, the classic “reroute and wait” playbook buys less resilience than many planners assume. Inventories and strategic reserves can bridge a gap. They cannot rebuild a corridor.

If your network touches energy, agri-inputs, chemicals, or packaging polymers — where are the single points of failure you still treat as background noise?
```
