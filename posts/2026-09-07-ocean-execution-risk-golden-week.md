# 2026-09-07 — Ocean capacity vs execution risk (pre–Golden Week)

**Angle:** September ocean freight looks looser on paper after an early U.S. import peak, but execution risk is rising. Typhoon-driven Asia port congestion, Panama Canal draft/slot cuts, and ~49 East–West blank sailings (Drewry; ~60% Trans-Pacific eastbound) shrink recovery options. Pre–China National Day (Oct 1–7) cargo plus storm backlogs will compete for space — a booking confirmation is not cargo on water. Combines unused “early peak tapering vs execution risk” and “blank sailings / Golden Week” candidates; distinct from Mundra empties, Dutch strikes, Panama-slot-only, and Typhoon Dolphin posts.

## Post copy

```
Ocean capacity looks available this September. Execution capacity is another story.

U.S. import volumes are easing after an early peak — NRF’s Global Port Tracker sees about 2.16 million TEUs in September, down from August. On paper, the Asia–U.S. fleet can handle that. In practice, typhoon-driven congestion at Shanghai and Ningbo, Panama Canal draft and slot cuts, and roughly 49 blank sailings across East–West trades through late September are stripping out recovery options. Drewry puts about 60% of those blanks on the Trans-Pacific eastbound alone.

A booking confirmation is no longer a guarantee of cargo on water. If your planned vessel is delayed, omits the origin, or can’t take the box, the next workable sailing may leave later, use another gateway, or add a transshipment — just as pre–Golden Week cargo and storm backlogs compete for the same space before China’s National Day holiday (Oct 1–7).

If a key Asia–U.S. shipment slips this month, what’s your Plan B sailing — and have you stress-tested it for weight limits, equipment, and cutoff timing?
```
