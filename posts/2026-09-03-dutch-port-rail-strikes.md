```
Tomorrow Europe’s largest gateway stops handling ships — five days later, its rail hinterland may stop too.

On 4 September, Dutch port unions are staging a nationwide stoppage across Rotterdam, Amsterdam and Zeeland. From about 11:15 to 19:00, no vessels will be handled: loading, unloading, cargo securing, inspections and towage are all off. Major Rotterdam terminals have already warned that truck gates and rail shuttle handling will shut earlier and later than the official window. Congestion typically builds before the stoppage and after restart — not only during it.

Then on 9 September, a separate 24-hour national rail action is set to hit ProRail and DB Cargo Nederland. That puts Betuweroute freight and Germany-bound corridors at risk just as boxes cleared from the quay need inland capacity. For Asia–Europe peak season cargo already absorbing Asia weather delays and blank-sailing pressure, a waterside halt followed by a hinterland halt is a compounded schedule problem, not a one-day inconvenience.

If your European routing still treats Rotterdam as always-available capacity — what are you moving, holding, or diverting before Friday and again before Wednesday?
```
