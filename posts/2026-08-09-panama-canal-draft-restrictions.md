**Angle:** ACP is tightening Neopanamax maximum draft again as Gatun Lake levels fall under a strengthening El Niño — 48.0 ft from Aug 26, then 47.5 ft from Sep 3. Daily transit slots stay for now; payload on the largest ships does not. Distinct from Rhine inland drought; climate is becoming a capacity constraint on a global ocean chokepoint amid rising U.S.–Asia energy flows and lengthening queues.

**Short copy:**
```
The Panama Canal isn’t closed — but the largest ships are being asked to carry less.

As Gatun Lake levels keep falling under a strengthening El Niño, the Panama Canal Authority is cutting Neopanamax maximum draft again: 48.0 feet from August 26, then 47.5 feet from September 3. Daily transit slots stay intact for now. Payload doesn’t. Containerships, LNG carriers, and tankers either leave cargo behind, lighten loads, or look for expensive workarounds — including landbridge moves across the isthmus.

This is not 2023–24 yet. But queues are already stretching past six days, U.S.–Asia energy flows have increased pressure after Hormuz disruptions, and climate is no longer a seasonal footnote in network design. Water is becoming a capacity constraint on one of the world’s most important trade arteries.

If your peak-season plan still treats Panama as a reliable deep-draft corridor — what are you adjusting before late August?
```
