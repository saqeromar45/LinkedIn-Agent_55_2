# 2026-08-23 — Jones Act waiver: Vessel Availability Request era

**Angle:** DHS extended the Jones Act national-defense waiver through November 15, 2026, but from August 17 foreign-flag coastwise voyages require a pre-voyage Vessel Availability Request to DOW/MARAD, a MARAD U.S.-flag market survey (typically 24 hours), and DOW authorization. Covered energy/fertilizer flows still depend on foreign tonnage under Hormuz pressure; waiver is no longer a standing blanket exemption. Distinct from Aug 5 Hormuz commodity-shock theme and Aug 21 Canada tariff theme.

The Jones Act waiver just got harder to use — not easier.

DHS extended the national-defense waiver through November 15, keeping foreign-flag vessels available for covered coastwise moves of energy, fertilizer, and related commodities. But the operational story changed on August 17: before any foreign-flag voyage, shippers must file a Vessel Availability Request with the Department of War and MARAD. MARAD runs a market survey so U.S.-flag operators can bid first — typically within 24 hours — and DOW must authorize the voyage before cargo moves.

That shift matters more than the 90-day headline. Domestic fertilizer and energy networks still rely on foreign tonnage while Hormuz pressure keeps supply tight. Lead time is now a compliance variable, not just a freight variable. Voyage planning that assumed a standing blanket exemption now needs pre-clearance, documentation discipline, and a realistic buffer for MARAD review.

If your coastwise plan still treats the Jones Act waiver as automatic — what Vessel Availability Request process are you building before your next load window?
