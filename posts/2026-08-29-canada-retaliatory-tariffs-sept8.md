# Canada retaliatory tariffs — September 8 two-way wall

**Date:** 2026-08-29  
**Angle:** Canada’s counter-tariffs (15/25/50%) take effect 12:01 a.m. Sept 8 on ~$27.6B of U.S.-origin imports across 700+ products; in-transit exemption if documented; two-sided North American tariff regime — distinct from 2026-08-21 Section 338 U.S. entry-date post.  
**Image:** `assets/canada-retaliatory-tariffs-sept8.png`

## LinkedIn copy

```
The U.S.–Canada tariff wall is about to close from both sides.

On September 8, Canada’s retaliatory surtaxes take effect — 15%, 25%, and 50% rates matched dollar-for-dollar against U.S. Section 338 and 232 measures, covering roughly $27.6 billion in U.S.-origin imports across steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, and electronics. Goods already in transit on that date can escape the levy if properly documented. Everything else entering Canada after 12:01 a.m. faces a new landed-cost reality.

For North American shippers, this is no longer a one-way compliance problem. U.S. brands selling into Canada, Canadian manufacturers dependent on U.S. inputs, and 3PLs routing across the border all need refreshed HS mapping, origin certification, and inventory positioning before the clock hits. In-transit exemptions reward those who moved early; everyone else is redesigning sourcing and fulfillment under a two-sided tariff regime.

If your cross-border playbook still only models duties on Canadian goods entering the U.S. — what are you changing before September 8?
```
