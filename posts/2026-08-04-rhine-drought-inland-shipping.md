## Ready-to-post copy

Europe’s busiest inland trade artery just hit a level not seen since records began in 1880.

At Kaub — the Rhine choke point for cargo heading into southern Germany and Switzerland — barge clearance fell to roughly 21 centimeters. When the river runs that shallow, barges sail light. In extreme cases, it can take four vessels to move what one full load used to carry. Freight rates spike, lead times stretch, and chemicals, fuels, agriproducts, and containers all feel the squeeze at once.

This isn’t a one-week weather story. Low-water events on the Rhine have become far more frequent over the past decade, and rail and road cannot fully absorb the lost barge capacity. For shippers and manufacturers, resilience now means buffer inventory where it earns its keep, multi-modal fallbacks locked in before the crisis, and visibility that flags inland constraints as early as ocean delays.

How are you redesigning European inland flows for drought seasons that keep arriving earlier — and lasting longer?
