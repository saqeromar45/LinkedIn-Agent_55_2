# 2026-08-07 — U.S. trucking capacity: a supply-side squeeze

**Angle:** U.S. truckload rates and tender rejections are rising into peak season less from a demand boom and more from structural capacity exit — CDL/English-proficiency enforcement, noncompliant school and ELD crackdowns, immigration-related removals, and fleets that under-invested during the downturn. Soft-market planning assumptions for Q3–Q4 no longer hold.

**Image:** `assets/us-trucking-capacity-tightening.png`

**Status:** published as `urn:li:share:7491434017192484864`

**Short copy:**
```
The U.S. truckload rebound isn’t a demand story. It’s a capacity story.

Freight demand is still uneven — yet spot and contract rates are climbing, tender rejections are staying elevated, and carriers are describing tightness they haven’t seen since 2021. What changed isn’t that every warehouse suddenly needs more trucks. It’s that trucks and drivers are leaving the market: stricter CDL and English-proficiency enforcement, crackdowns on noncompliant schools and ELDs, immigration-related removals, and fleets that spent years not replacing equipment.

That matters heading into peak. When capacity exits for regulatory and structural reasons, it doesn’t snap back when rates rise. Routing guides get thinner. Spot premiums stick. And cargo theft keeps adding another layer of risk to everyday freight — not just high-value electronics.

If your Q3–Q4 plan still assumes soft truckload markets from the last downturn — what are you revising now?
```
